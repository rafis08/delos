# Delos launch and marketplace playbook

## Objective

Delos wins when compatible musicians get into a room together. The north-star metric is **confirmed compatible sessions per weekly active musician**, supported by the funnel: eligible profiles viewed → connection sent → mutual match → first message → proposal → acceptance → confirmed session.

## Launch one dense market

Start with Detroit and define a launch zone rather than opening national acquisition. Do not expand until most activated users can see at least five eligible, recently active musicians and core instrument/genre clusters have roughly twenty contactable candidates.

Recruit supply by role. Track coverage for vocals, guitar, bass, drums, keys, and production separately. When a cluster is thin, recruit that role through rehearsal studios, music schools, instrument shops, venues, open mics, and local scene organizers. Never hide low inventory behind a paid upgrade.

## Initial customer and message

Lead acquisition with the urgent job: **Fill the missing role in your band.** The first paying segment is an active bandleader or committed musician recruiting now. Keep casual jams, session work, and paid gigs in the product, but do not use one generic campaign for every intent.

## Free and Amplified boundary

Free members receive complete compatibility controls, matching, mutual messaging, rehearsal proposals, one active Band Call, and three media uploads. Amplified sells acceleration and control: incoming likes, unlimited connections, rewind, incognito mode, ten media uploads, weekly visibility boost, and three simultaneous active Band Calls.

The current Stripe product remains $9.99/month until a controlled price test is ready. Test $7.99 monthly against $9.99 only after a user has sufficient local inventory. Add a $49.99 annual option only after its Stripe price, native-store equivalents, entitlement restoration, and regional display are complete. Never display a price that checkout cannot honor.

## Activation and retention

Activation requires a complete profile, a performance sample, five eligible local candidates, and one meaningful action. Notifications should advance real work: a relevant Band Call, a reply, a proposal, readiness, or preparation—not generic engagement.

After a match, guide users into a first message, a three-song session proposal, two-person readiness, calendar placement, and the Band Room. After the scheduled time, privately ask whether the session happened. Keep successful bands engaged through setlists, tasks, notes, repeat sessions, and future recruiting.

## Trust and safety operations

Factual readiness signals may include verified email, a performance sample, transportation, appropriate equipment, recent activity, and confirmed sessions. Do not launch public star ratings. Session confirmations remain private until there is enough data and a reviewed anti-retaliation policy.

Assign a human report owner before public launch. Define severity levels, response targets, evidence retention, repeat-offender handling, appeals, emergency escalation, and account-deletion procedures. Review report and block rates per conversation, proposal, and confirmed session.

## Weekly operating dashboard

- Eligible profiles per activated user, segmented by metro, role, genre, and intent
- Profile and performance-sample completion
- Connection-to-match and match-to-first-message conversion
- Reply rate and time to first reply
- Proposal, acceptance, readiness, and confirmed-session conversion
- Week-one and month-one retention by metro
- Active Band Calls and application response time
- Paid conversion only among users with sufficient eligible inventory
- Reports, blocks, and moderation response time

## Expansion gate

Open another metro only when Detroit has repeatable supply acquisition, healthy response rates, confirmed sessions, a staffed moderation workflow, and evidence that users retain value after forming a group. Code cannot solve marketplace density; city operations and partnerships own that outcome.
